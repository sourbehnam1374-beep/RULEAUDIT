# Literature context for RuleAudit

This document places RuleAudit's calculations beside established clinical-prediction and sensitivity-analysis methods. It is not a systematic review and does not validate the software or its bundled rule encodings.

## Clinical-prediction model lifecycle

Clinical prediction research normally distinguishes model development, validation, and evaluation of clinical impact. Reporting and risk-of-bias frameworks such as TRIPOD and PROBAST address studies conducted with participant data and outcomes.

RuleAudit has a narrower target. Its first five diagnostics evaluate the behavior of an implemented deterministic score under stated synthetic-input assumptions without requiring individual outcome labels. This is best described as **outcome-free structural screening**, not “data-free validation”: the rule source, encoding, bounds, sampler, seed, and sample size are all analysis inputs.

RuleAudit does not replace any development, external-validation, calibration, impact, fairness, safety, or regulatory assessment. A clean report is neither necessary nor sufficient evidence of clinical utility.

Authoritative framework sites:

- [TRIPOD Statement](https://www.tripod-statement.org/)
- [PROBAST](https://www.probast.org/)

## Driver-frequency, correlation, and VIF diagnostics

Firing rates, correlation matrices, and variance inflation factors are standard descriptive or linear-dependence summaries. RuleAudit applies them to driver-contribution columns generated by a synthetic sweep.

Their interpretation is conditional on the sweep. High correlation or VIF can motivate review of overlapping rule expressions, but it does not establish causal redundancy, coefficient instability in an unfitted fixed score, or clinical invalidity. A zero firing rate in the sweep does not prove that activation is impossible outside the configured support.

## OAT and Sobol sensitivity analysis

One-at-a-time (OAT) analysis varies one input while holding other inputs at a selected seed. It is easy to inspect but local to that seed. Variance-based Sobol indices summarize contributions to output variance over a specified input distribution and can represent interaction contributions through total-order indices.

Version 0.2.1 uses two distinct analysis targets:

- the random sweep can use a configured correlated `joint_sampler`;
- OAT uses fixed seed cases, while Sobol sampling uses independent uniform draws over the declared `InputSpec` bounds.

The Sobol results therefore describe the configured independent rectangular envelope, not the correlated random-sweep sampler and not a clinical population. They do not by themselves establish measurement-error harm, outcome importance, or patient-level risk.

Methodological background includes Saltelli and colleagues' work on variance-based sensitivity analysis. The package uses SALib for the numerical implementation.

## Driver-pattern multiplicity

RuleAudit's Test 5 counts distinct sampled driver-contribution patterns at each total score. The public API retains the legacy name `identifiability`, but the calculation is not a classical statistical-identifiability test.

Pattern counts and `n_patterns / n_cases` depend on the rule encoding, sampler support, sample size, seed, and score weights. The `LOW-PATTERN-RATIO` and `HIGH-PATTERN-MULTIPLICITY` flags are software review heuristics. There is no established evidence that these thresholds predict discrimination, calibration, transportability, or external-validation consistency.

This diagnostic should be reported as descriptive within-total compression or pattern multiplicity, suitable for code review and hypothesis generation.

## Exploratory MDL comparison

Minimum Description Length (MDL) provides a general model-selection perspective that balances a representation of the model with a representation of residual data. RuleAudit's optional module borrows that perspective but implements a specific, simplified in-sample comparison:

- the rule code length is supplied by the caller;
- outcome uncertainty is summarized conditionally on the score total;
- an L1-logistic comparator is selected from a fixed regularization grid;
- no cross-validation, uncertainty analysis, or validated clinical decision threshold is provided.

The resulting bit difference is an experimental software output. It must not be treated as evidence that a score should be retained, replaced, or deployed.

## Orthopedic worked examples

The bundled examples illustrate the workflow. They are not independently certified clinical reference implementations.

### GAP score

The GAP score was introduced for mechanical-complication risk after adult spinal deformity surgery. The package contains an implementation of its published point structure for worked-example purposes.

External studies have reported variable discrimination. Kwan and colleagues analyzed 159 patients from 272 enrolled and reported an AUC of 0.60 (95% CI 0.50–0.70) for mechanical complications and 0.66 (95% CI 0.53–0.78) for revision. A later systematic review summarized 32 reports involving more than 5,700 patients and reported AUC values ranging from 0.53 to 0.86.

These clinical findings provide context only. RuleAudit's synthetic correlations, Sobol indices, and pattern counts do not explain or predict the between-study variation.

Primary links:

- [GAP development paper](https://doi.org/10.2106/JBJS.16.01594)
- [Kwan external validation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33079774/)
- [Systematic review of GAP performance](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41311503/)

### Lewinnek safe zone

The published Lewinnek safe zone defines a rectangular range for acetabular component inclination and anteversion. RuleAudit re-encodes that rectangle as four boundary-violation contributions so that it can be processed by the additive-score workflow. The resulting totals and pattern counts are properties of this author-created encoding, not additional claims made in the original publication.

Abdel and colleagues studied 9,784 primary total hip arthroplasties and reported that 120 of 206 dislocated hips were inside the Lewinnek safe zone. This is the proportion of observed dislocations located inside the zone; it is not an estimate of dislocation risk among all hips inside the zone and should not be compared with the synthetic proportion labelled `SAFE`. A systematic review by Seagrave and colleagues separately examined cup position and dislocation evidence.

Primary links:

- [Abdel et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26150264/)
- [Seagrave et al. systematic review](https://doi.org/10.1080/17453674.2016.1251255)

### SHIVA SPIN-THA

The SHIVA module is an illustrative draft-rule encoding supplied for software demonstration. Its inclusion, generated report, or comparison with the other examples is not peer-reviewed validation of SHIVA and should not be cited as clinical evidence.

## Evidence boundary

Appropriate claims are limited to reproducible descriptions of the implemented rule under an explicitly stated synthetic analysis. Claims about prediction, calibration, causation, mechanisms, clinical validity, transportability, or safety require separate patient-level evidence and appropriate study design.
